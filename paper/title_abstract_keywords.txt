Title
Same ceiling, different machinery: mechanistic interpretability of a text language model trained to read cells as sentences

Abstract
Cell-sentence foundation models represent a transcriptome as ranked gene names. Their internal biological structure needs to be distinguished from information already available in that input. We audit C2S-Scale, a Gemma-2 language model at 2B and 27B parameters, using sparse autoencoders, feature ablations, geometry, steering and controlled readouts. Validation includes complete multiple-testing families, empirical annotation floors, measured-expression controls, input-matched baselines and comparisons at matched dictionary capacity and relative depth. The 2B atlas contains 239 616 features across all 26 layers. Named ontology or regulon terms label 63.8%, compared with a 34.4% frequency-matched random-gene floor on average. Two late layers do not exceed their matched floor. Untrained directions also carry extensive labels. At seven matched depths, 27B has greater held-out inactivity and lower raw annotation, while its annotation excess is not uniformly lower. Half of the layer-zero decoder directions have a similar direction at the final layer. Gene geometry exceeds a co-expression-matched null, and internal steering changes the model's gene readout. These are model-level findings rather than evidence of regulation inside cells. Cell-phase readouts show no resolved gain over input-rank baselines: for frozen K562-to-RPE1 transfer, the model-minus-rank difference is -0.001, with a paired 95% interval of -0.013 to 0.011. The known-target assay is unsigned and must be interpreted together with its expression positive control and target coverage. The resulting atlas supports inspection of cell-state programmes and generation of hypotheses; claims about regulatory direction require independent biological evidence.

Keywords
foundation models; interpretability; sparse autoencoders; single-cell transcriptomics; model evaluation; causal analysis
